│       │ The UK's AI regulatory framework, outlined in the 2023 "A Pro-Innovation Approach to Artificial Intelligence" paper, does not create a single, standalone AI regulator or a new, comprehensive AI-specific law. Instead, it adopts a sector-specific approach, empowering existing regulators (such as the Information Commissioner's Office, the Competition and Markets Authority, and others) to apply their current powers and expertise to AI-related issues within their respective domains. │    ├─ sub-question: What are the key principles that guide the UK's AI regulatory framework?
│    └─ answer:
│       │ The UK's AI regulatory framework is guided by five key principles: it must be safe, secure, and robust; it must be designed and used in a way that is fair and non-discriminatory; it must be transparent and explainable; it must be accountable, with human oversight; and it must be contestable, allowing individuals to challenge AI-driven decisions. These principles were outlined in the UK government's "Pro-Innovation Approach to AI Regulation" white paper published in March 2023. The framework takes a context-specific approach, distributing regulatory responsibilities across existing sectoral bodies rather than creating a single AI regulator.
