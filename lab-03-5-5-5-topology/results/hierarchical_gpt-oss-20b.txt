     - The U.S. framework is **sector‑specific**: existing statutes (FDA, SEC, NHTSA) impose AI‑related rules within their domains, while federal initiatives set broad policy directions but do not yet constitute a single, comprehensive AI law.

     - The EU relies on **national competent authorities** and a **European AI Board** to coordinate oversight, conduct audits, and enforce compliance.  
     - In the U.S., enforcement is **distributed** among the agencies that already regulate the relevant sector (FDA, SEC, NHTSA, etc.). No single agency oversees all AI applications.
  
  3. **Penalties**  
     - The EU imposes **monetary fines** (up to €30 million or 6 % of worldwide turnover) and can ban products from the market.  
     - U.S. penalties vary by agency and sector; they may include recalls, enforcement actions, or other sanctions specific to the regulatory body.
