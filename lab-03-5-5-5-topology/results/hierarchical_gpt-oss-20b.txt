│    └─ answer:
│       │ The EU AI Act classifies AI systems into four risk categories: **unacceptable risk** (prohibited, e.g., social scoring by governments), **high risk** (subject to mandatory conformity assessment, data governance, documentation, human oversight, and post‑market monitoring), **limited risk** (requires transparency disclosures such as informing users that they are interacting with AI), and **minimal risk** (no specific obligations). High‑risk systems must undergo a rigorous conformity assessment, register with the national authority, and maintain a technical documentation dossier, while limited‑risk systems must provide clear, accessible information to users. Unacceptable‑risk systems are banned outright, and minimal‑risk systems face no regulatory requirements.
│    Leaf 1.2 (wall 14.87s)
│    ├─ sub-question: What enforcement mechanisms, including penalties and compliance monitoring, are specified in the EU AI Act?
│    └─ answer:
│       │ The EU AI Act establishes a compliance monitoring regime that requires providers of high‑risk AI systems to register with national authorities, conduct conformity assessments, and maintain technical documentation for audit purposes. National supervisory authorities are empowered to conduct inspections, request evidence, and impose administrative sanctions, while the European Artificial Intelligence Board coordinates cross‑border enforcement and shares best practices. Penalties for non‑compliance can reach up to €30 million or 6 % of a company’s worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, and may include temporary or permanent bans on the use of the AI system.
